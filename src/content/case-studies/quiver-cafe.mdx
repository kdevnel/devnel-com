## The problem

Quiver Café is a social-impact café and waffle cart in Woodstock, Cape Town,
and part of the Quiver Foundation. Its two sites, quivercafe.co.za and
liegewaffles.co.za, were static pages.

Static pages are fast and cheap to host, but every content change meant editing
the pages themselves, so updates needed someone comfortable working in code.
The business also wanted to sell online eventually, which static pages can't do
on their own.

## Constraints

- **Keep what was good about static.** Performance and running costs couldn't get
  worse.
- **Anyone on the team has to be able to manage the content**, without a
  developer and without breaking anything.
- **The sites should stay AI-friendly to edit.**
- **The powerful parts have to be safe.** Taking payments and arranging delivery
  are real commitments for a small business, so they had to be easy to switch
  on when the business is ready, and not before.
- **Running costs and maintenance have to stay low.** It's a small social-impact
  business run by a non-profit, and the site shouldn't become a cost centre.

## What I built

**A rebuild of both sites on Astro and Sanity, deployed on Cloudflare Workers.**
I introduced content editing. Everything the team needs to change is editable in
Sanity Studio, while the sites stay AI-friendly to edit and tracked through GitHub.

**Structure that protects the sites.** Content now has a defined shape in
Sanity, rather than living in the page markup. That gives the sites better
resilience against unwanted breakage or changes: editing content can't break the
layout around it.

**Online ordering, built and ready.** Payments go through Yoco and delivery is
arranged through Uber Direct. Orders and live status updates are sent to a
dedicated Slack channel for order management and tacking. The business can switch
this on with a single toggle in the Sanity dashboard whenever they're ready: no
developer and no manual deploy.

## Stack

Astro and TypeScript for the sites, Sanity for content and settings, Cloudflare
Workers for hosting, Yoco for payments, Uber Direct for delivery, and Slack for
order notifications and tracking.

## Results

- **Performance is still top notch**, as it was with the static pages. Lighthouse
  scores in the high 90s, with some improvements in asset rendering.
- **SEO, OpenGraph and schema is improved.**
- **Accessibility is improved and automated axe checks in CI.**
- **Anyone on the team can manage the sites** in Sanity Studio.
- The founder can continue to make deeper design and layout changes with AI.
- **Ongoing and hosting costs are close to nothing**, compared with a full hosted
  CMS website that could cost upwards of $300 per year.
- **Both sites are fully live**, and online ordering is ready to switch on with
  one toggle.

## What I'd do differently

- Provide some specific agent identities and skills in the codebase to make
  AI-assisted changes more predictable for anyone editing the site.
