## The problem

IGrow is a Cape Town property investment group with companies covering
investments, rentals, real estate and removals. Viewings for rental properties
were booked by hand through the contact centre. Every booking had to go through
the contact centre, which made it a bottleneck, and management had little
visibility of what was being booked, by whom, and what happened next.

The rentals site had its own problem: it was slow. GTmetrix graded it E or F.

## Constraints

- The tool had to work hand in hand with Zoho CRM: linking user accounts,
  managing leads and recording every booking against the right Zoho module.
- Three very different groups needed different levels of access: contact-centre
  staff, rental agents and management.
- Listings come from PropData, so the site and the booking tool both had to stay
  in sync with 200+ properties without anyone copying data by hand.
- The rentals site was a test for what was possible with the other company
  sites. This led to a timeline of only 3 weeks of solo work.
- The website was hosted on a congested server that was further limiting
  performance and needed migrating.

## What I built

**An internal viewing-booking tool, tightly coupled with Zoho CRM.** It links
user accounts, manages leads and records each booking against the relevant Zoho
module, so the CRM stays the single place to look.

**Role-based access** for contact-centre staff, agents and management, so each
group sees and does what it needs to.

**Self-service booking for prospective tenants.** People can book a viewing
themselves instead of waiting on the contact centre. That took people out of
the bottleneck and improved booking rates.

**A rebuilt rentals.igrow.co.za**: a custom, performance-first theme, proper
caching, and a PropData integration that keeps 200+ listings in sync on a
schedule.

**The infrastructure around it**: Linux servers running NGINX and Caddy, and
GitHub Actions pipelines that run smoke and synthetic tests before every deploy.

The next step, scoped but not built, was an agent roster generator: a rules
engine and real Google Maps travel times to replace manual scheduling and fit
more viewings into each day.

## Stack

PHP and WordPress for the rentals site and PropData. TypeScript for the booking
tool. Zoho CRM, Flow, SalesIQ API and Google Maps APIs alongside both in
relevant place. Managed Hosting for WordPress, NGINX on Linux for the booking
tool. GitHub Actions for CI/CD on both.

## Results

- **25+ contact-centre staff, 10 rental agents and management** use the booking
  tool.
- **30+ bookings a day** across 200 properties.
- **GTmetrix went from E/F to A/B** on the rentals site, and server load fell
  even as traffic grew.

## What I'd do differently

- Fully finalise the web component design system ahead of the build. This would
  have made consistency easier from the beginning in a quickly-changing
  environment.
- As a test site, I was conservative with how I handled the frontend. I opted
  for a traditional PHP WordPress theme frontend with a parent theme dependency.
  A fully custom theme would have provided me more control.
